# Risk Assessment of Microplastics in Drinking Water: Current Position

## Scope of the assessment

This assessment reviews exposure estimates and hazard data for microplastics in drinking water and asks whether current concentrations are associated with a demonstrated health risk. It considers 52 studies published between 2016 and 2023.

## Exposure

Estimated adult intake from drinking water ranges from 0.1 to 4 particles per kilogram of body weight per day, depending on the method used to count particles below 50 micrometres. The range spans more than an order of magnitude, which is itself a finding: the measurement methods are not yet comparable.

## Hazard

No epidemiological study has reported a health outcome associated with drinking-water microplastic exposure. Toxicological studies report effects at doses far above environmental concentrations. The margin between the lowest effect dose in rodents and estimated human intake is roughly one thousandfold.

## Data gaps

Three gaps prevent a firm conclusion. First, particle counts below 10 micrometres are unreliable. Second, most studies measure particles rather than mass, so dose cannot be compared across studies. Third, no cohort has followed exposed and unexposed populations long enough to detect chronic outcomes.

## Position

On current evidence, microplastics in drinking water at measured concentrations do not constitute an established health risk. The absence of evidence is not proof of safety, and the assessment recommends standardising measurement methods before risk is re-evaluated.
