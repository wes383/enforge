# Urinary Microplastic Concentrations and Inflammatory Markers in a Prospective Cohort

## Abstract

We measured microplastic particle concentrations in repeated urine samples from 1,240 adults enrolled in a regional cohort and tested associations with three inflammatory markers. Concentrations ranged from below the detection limit to 84 particles per millilitre, with a median of 3.1 particles per millilitre. Higher concentrations were associated with elevated C-reactive protein, with a 0.12 milligram per litre increase per interquartile range. The design cannot establish causality.

## Methods

Participants provided three samples over 18 months. Particles were counted by micro-Raman spectroscopy with a lower size cutoff of 5 micrometres. Blanks were processed alongside every batch and 11 percent of the blank-corrected values fell below the detection limit. Inflammatory markers were measured by standard immunoassay.

## Results

C-reactive protein rose by 0.12 milligrams per litre per interquartile range of particle concentration, with a 95 percent confidence interval from 0.04 to 0.20. Interleukin-6 showed a smaller association that did not reach conventional significance. Fibrinogen showed no association. The associations were unchanged after adjustment for age, smoking, body mass index and diet quality.

## Interpretation

The association is consistent with a low-grade inflammatory response, but the magnitudes are small. A 0.12 milligram per litre change in C-reactive protein is well inside normal variation for an individual. We did not observe any association with clinical events, and the cohort was not powered to detect one.

## Limitations

Particle counting is sensitive to laboratory contamination. We cannot distinguish particle origin, so drinking water exposure cannot be separated from food and air exposure. The cohort is regionally concentrated, with 61 percent of participants drawn from two cities.
