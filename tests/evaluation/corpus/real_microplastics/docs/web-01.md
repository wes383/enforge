# Consumer Testing Report: Microplastics in Municipal Drinking Water

## What we tested

We collected 259 tap water samples from 14 municipal systems and analysed them by infrared spectroscopy with a 10 micrometre cutoff. Ninety percent of samples contained at least one plastic particle. The maximum concentration was 61 particles per litre.

## What we found

Bottled water from the same regions contained 22 particles per litre on average, compared with 9 particles per litre in tap water. The most common polymers were polyethylene terephthalate, polypropylene and polystyrene. Fibres accounted for 63 percent of counted particles.

## What this means

The presence of plastic particles in drinking water is widespread and is not confined to a single region or supplier. Consumers cannot remove particles by boiling, which concentrates them slightly. A filter rated below 10 micrometres reduced counts by 84 percent in our tests.

## What we did not test

We did not measure health outcomes and we did not estimate dose in milligrams. Our counts are particle counts. We cannot say from this work whether the concentrations we measured have any health consequence, and readers should not interpret detection as evidence of harm.
