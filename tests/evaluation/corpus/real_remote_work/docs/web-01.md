# Longitudinal Evidence on Remote Work and Team Output

## Study design

The Workplace Analytics Lab followed 4,200 knowledge workers across 18 firms from 2019 to 2022. Output was measured from version-control activity, ticket throughput and billed client hours rather than self-report. The panel retained 3,764 workers through the final wave, a retention rate of 89.6 percent.

## Individual output

Workers who moved to remote arrangements produced 12 percent more completed tickets per month than their office-based baseline. The gain persisted after controlling for role, tenure and seasonality. Median turnaround time for individual tasks fell from 3.4 days to 2.9 days.

## Coordination cost

Cross-team collaboration dropped 18 percent. The share of work involving three or more teams fell from 27 percent to 22 percent. Feedback latency for junior employees rose 34 percent, from a median of 5.1 hours to 6.8 hours. Senior employees showed no measurable change in feedback latency.

## Interpretation

The data support a split conclusion. Remote arrangements raise the throughput of work that can be done alone and reduce the throughput of work that depends on rapid coordination. Firms that measured only individual ticket throughput recorded a productivity gain; firms that measured project completion recorded a smaller gain of 3 percent.

## Limitations

The panel covers knowledge work in software and consulting only. Manufacturing and clinical settings are absent. Output metrics were normalised per firm because the 18 firms used different tracking systems, which introduces an estimated error of plus or minus 4 percent.
