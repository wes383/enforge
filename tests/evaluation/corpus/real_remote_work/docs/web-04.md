# Industry Survey Report: Remote Work and Output

## Method

The survey collected responses from 380 managers at firms with more than 200 employees. Respondents were recruited through an industry newsletter and asked to estimate the change in output and employee satisfaction since their remote programme began. The response rate was 9 percent.

## Headline numbers

Managers reported a 20 percent increase in output and a 35 percent increase in employee satisfaction. Seventy-two percent said the arrangement would continue permanently. The median programme age was 14 months.

## How the numbers were produced

Respondents estimated changes from memory rather than from records. Only 38 of the 380 respondents cited a system that tracked output before and after the transition. The report does not adjust for role mix, tenure or the sector shift that occurred during the same period.

## Author caveats

The authors note that the sample is self-selected and that managers who respond to a survey about their own programme have an incentive to report success. They describe the 20 percent figure as a perception of change rather than a measured change.

## Relation to other evidence

The survey is the most cited source for a large positive effect, but it measures perception. It should not be combined with studies that measure output directly, since the two quantities are not on the same scale.
