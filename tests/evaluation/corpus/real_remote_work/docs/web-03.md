# A Field Experiment in a Call Centre: Task Interdependence Decides the Effect

## Design

A randomised field experiment assigned 1,100 customer service agents to remote or office work for eight months. Productivity was measured as resolved cases per hour, which the firm tracked automatically. The trial ran from January to August 2023 with no attrition in measurement.

## Results by task type

Agents working remotely resolved 8 percent more cases per hour on independent tasks. On coordination-heavy tasks, defined as cases requiring handoff to two or more colleagues, remote agents resolved 4 percent fewer cases per hour. The two effects are statistically distinguishable at conventional thresholds.

## Absence and retention

Sick days fell 21 percent among remote agents. Voluntary attrition fell 12 percent over the eight months. The firm estimated a saving of 0.6 full-time equivalents per 100 agents from reduced absence.

## Mechanism

The authors argue the result reflects substitution rather than a general productivity change. Remote agents spend less time in unplanned interruptions, which helps independent work, and less time in synchronous problem solving, which hurts coordinated work. Neither effect is evidence of effort change.

## Practical reading

The headline depends entirely on how the task mix is weighted. A firm whose work is 80 percent independent should expect a gain near 6 percent. A firm whose work is 40 percent coordination should expect a gain near 3 percent. Reporting a single blended number hides the mechanism that produced it.
